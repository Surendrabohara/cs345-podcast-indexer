# Recording 2: Two-person interview (about 3 minutes)

You voice both people (invented): HOST = Rohan Pandey, GUEST = Maya Ortiz. Change your voice a little between them and pause between turns.

HOST: Welcome to Mountain Water. I am Rohan Pandey, and my guest today is Maya Ortiz, who studies glacier melt.

GUEST: Thank you, Rohan. It is good to be here.

HOST: Maya, why should a farmer in a village care about glacier melt?

GUEST: Because the glacier works like a bank account. In the warm months it pays water into the river. If the glacier shrinks, the account runs low, and the farmer feels it first in the dry season.

HOST: So this is about irrigation.

GUEST: Yes. Irrigation is where it shows up most. Many villages use small canals to move river water to their fields. When the river drops, the canals at the end of the line get nothing.

HOST: What can a village do?

GUEST: Three things. First, share water by a fair schedule, so that every field gets some. Second, fix leaking canals, because a lot of water is lost before it reaches the crops. Third, grow crops that need less water in the dry months.

HOST: Does measuring the river help?

GUEST: A great deal. If a village writes down the river level every week, in a few years they can see the trend. Then they can plan irrigation instead of guessing.

HOST: Maya Ortiz, thank you for explaining glacier melt in such simple words.

GUEST: Thank you, Rohan.

HOST: This was Rohan Pandey for Mountain Water.
